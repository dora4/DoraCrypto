## A Disturbing Set of Statistics

Before diving deeper, let’s look at some figures:

**Global data breach scale:** In the first half of 2026, publicly disclosed major data breaches affected more than 500 million personal records. The Pentagon’s personnel system was hacked, exposing information on 2.8 million service members, and the data was unencrypted. US firm CareCloud confirmed that complete medical records of 3.7 million patients were stolen. Five major commercial banks in South Korea suffered a synchronized AI-powered hacker attack; detailed financial data of 25,000 customers was stolen over 25 hours without the banks detecting the intrusion.

**Domestic regulatory enforcement:** The special campaign “Clear the Web · Rectify Chaos in AI Applications” removed over 5.61 million illegal and non-compliant pieces of information and investigated more than 49,000 violating accounts. Authorities have inspected and tested over 20,000 mobile apps, with more than 4,000 completing compliance rectification and over 400 being removed from app stores.

**AI’s hunger for data:** The global core AI industry has exceeded $2.52 trillion in value, while China’s market has surpassed 1.2 trillion yuan. Training large language models requires massive volumes of data — chat logs, usage habits, file contents and behavioral patterns. Where does this data come from? From the various apps you use every day.

Put these numbers together, and the conclusion is clear: **Your data is being collected, traded and monetized on a massive scale, without your knowledge and without any benefit to you.**

## The Essence of “Cloud Storage”: An Unequal Bargain

Let’s take a closer look at cloud storage.

Baidu Netdisk, iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox — the core deal is the same: you hand over your files to the platform for safekeeping, and the platform offers you convenience: cross-device sync, anytime access and one-click sharing.

It sounds fair. But on closer inspection, this arrangement has severe flaws:

**First, you surrender physical control.** Your files end up on servers operated by a company. That company has its own staff, security systems and commercial interests. If servers are breached, insiders misuse access, or AI systems overstep permissions (OpenAI’s agents have proven this can happen), your files are exposed. And you will not be notified.

**Second, you feed a system that does not answer to you.** Major cloud drives perform “content review” and “security scanning” on your files. That means programs read your documents. How much they read, how deeply, and who receives the extracted information — you have no way of knowing, and no power to audit.

**Third, opaque cost structures.** Centralized cloud storage has nearly zero marginal cost. Storing an extra gigabyte adds almost no expense for the platform. Yet your subscription fees do not fall. Instead, free storage quotas keep shrinking, speed limits grow stricter, and membership fees keep rising. You feed your data to train the platform’s AI, then pay the platform to keep holding your data.

The math simply does not add up.

## The “Cost” of Data Breaches Is More Than Money

Many people only associate data breaches with credit card fraud. The reality is far more complex.

Take the South Korean bank hack: the stolen data on 25,000 customers included names, phone numbers, annual income and loan limits. On the dark web, this dataset may sell for only tens of thousands of US dollars — a small sum for hackers. But for each affected individual, the consequences can include:

- **Sophisticated targeted scams:** Armed with your income and loan details, scammers craft custom pitches. Phrases like “There is an anomaly with your loan at XX Bank and verification is required” become far more credible with specific figures.
- **Social engineering attacks:** With knowledge of your finances, attackers can launch targeted scams against people around you.
- **Long-term tracking risk:** Once this data enters criminal databases, it can be reused for years or even decades.

The CareCloud breach of 3.7 million patient records offers another example. Medical files contain not just names and social security numbers, but full treatment histories, medication records and mental health assessments. After exposure, this information may affect insurance premiums, job prospects and even personal relationships.

**The true cost of a data breach often emerges years later — by which point you can no longer trace where the leak occurred.**

## An Alternative Approach to Storage

With all these problems laid out, what is the solution?

DoraCloud aims to answer a fundamental question: **What if storage does not require trusting a third party at all?**

It is a cloud storage service for everyday users, supporting storage and management of both sensitive private data and ordinary files. It differs from mainstream cloud drives in three key ways:

**You retain data sovereignty.** Files are encrypted on your device before upload, and the encryption keys stay solely in your possession. Even if DoraCloud’s servers are compromised, attackers only obtain ciphertext — meaningless garbled data without the keys. This is a fundamental departure from mainstream cloud services where platforms hold your files after upload.

**Decentralized storage architecture.** It does not rely on a single data center. Files are split into fragments, encrypted and spread across multiple nodes. No single node can reconstruct the complete file. This eliminates the risk of a “master key” and removes the attack surface where breaching one server unlocks all your data.

**Partitioned management for private and ordinary files.** Your work documents and family photos can reside in the general zone for easy daily sync and sharing. Sensitive materials such as ID scans, bank credentials, medical reports and personal diaries go into the privacy zone, with independent encryption and access controls as an extra safeguard.

These three design choices follow one core principle: **Your data remains under your control, both physically and logically.**

## “Zero Trust” Is Not Distrust — It Is Respect

The IT industry has a concept called Zero Trust Architecture: never trust any node on the network by default; every access request requires verification.

DoraCloud brings this philosophy to personal storage — **trust no one by default, not even DoraCloud itself.**

This is not distrust toward the platform, but respect for user rights. Only you know best the value and sensitivity of your photos, documents and personal records. Entrusting their security to an unauditable third party is essentially a gamble.

The data security landscape in 2026 makes this clear: nothing is “100% secure,” from state-level systems down to your personal phone. The only viable step is to pull control over risk back into your own hands as much as possible.

DoraCloud does not promise absolute security — no system can. What it promises: **Between your data and all potential threats sits a lock that only you can open.**

This lock rests not on promises, but on mathematics.
