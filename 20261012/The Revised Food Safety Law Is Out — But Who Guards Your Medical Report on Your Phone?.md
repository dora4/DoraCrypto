# The Revised Food Safety Law Is Out — But Who Guards Your Medical Report on Your Phone?

There’s a news story many people scrolled past recently: the revised draft of the Food Safety Law is open for public comments.

The draft includes new provisions for **digital food labels**, requiring merchants on e-commerce platforms to ensure online product information matches physical goods, with harsher penalties for false labeling.

This is good news. At the very least, ingredient lists will be more reliable when you buy snacks.

Yet another question crosses my mind: what about your health data?

You had a physical exam last week. The report says your blood lipids are elevated? Your uric acid is borderline? One liver function marker came back abnormal? You snapped a photo of the report on your phone, telling yourself you’d review it later — and then never did.

That photo of your medical report sits quietly in your phone gallery, alongside food delivery screenshots, courier receipts and cat selfies.

**The most honest record of your body exists in one of the least secure ways possible.**

Full name, ID number, home address, bank card details, health status, rental records, major account passwords. With all of that, I’d rather not list what scammers could do. You already know.

## Food Safety Has Regulation. Phone Security Rides on Luck?

One phrase stuck with me from the revised Food Safety Law draft: **full-chain supervision**. Every step from raw materials, production and distribution to the dinner table must be traceable and auditable.

This logic makes sense. Food is tangible after all: you can see packaging, check labels, and hold parties accountable when something goes wrong.

What about the data on your phone?

It has no packaging, no labels. Most of the time you do not even know where it is stored, how much of it there is, or who has accessed it. Dozens of apps hold data on your device, each with its own storage rules, security standards and privacy policy. Scattering sensitive files across different apps is like stashing cash under your pillow, inside your shoe cabinet and behind the fridge — you cannot even keep track of all the hiding spots yourself.

And the threat landscape in 2026 is more severe than ever.

In September this year, an app named FomoPeek passed Apple’s App Store review. Version 1.0 was clean, but after updating to version 1.1, it deployed a kernel-level attack framework capable of bypassing iOS sandbox restrictions to read data from all apps on the device. This malicious code remained on Apple’s official app store for 12 days until Binance issued an urgent security alert.

Twelve days. More than enough time for a carefully crafted attack program to silently sweep everything on countless users’ phones.

## A Dedicated Space for What Matters Most

DoraBox is built on a simple idea: **the data on your phone that you cannot afford to lose, dare not leave exposed, yet must keep — deserves a dedicated place.**

Not your photo gallery, not notes, not WeChat favorites. It needs an encrypted, isolated space that only you can unlock.

Its core function is straightforward: **encrypted storage for private data.** Behind the word “encryption”, DoraBox implements solid safeguards:

**AES-256 military-grade encryption.** This is the standard widely adopted by global financial institutions and military bodies. It is not a mere promise of security; brute-forcing AES-256 is impractical under current computing power. Your data is protected by mathematics, not just a company’s security team.

**Zero-knowledge architecture.** DoraBox servers never receive or store your encryption keys. Keys are generated and kept locally on your device and never uploaded. That means even DoraBox’s own staff cannot open your secure vault. This is not a promise; it is baked into the technical architecture.

**Biometric quick unlock.** Fingerprint and facial recognition serve as convenient daily access methods. Your biometric data stays on your device locally and never travels to any third-party server.

**Decoy vault.** A clever feature: you can set a decoy password. Entering it opens a fake vault holding trivial files. If someone forces you to unlock your phone, you can pull up the decoy vault while your real sensitive data remains safe.

## What Belongs Inside the Vault?

Everyone’s list will differ. But if you do the same phone inventory I did, you will likely agree these categories belong in DoraBox:

**Identity documents:** Scans of ID cards, passports, driver’s licenses and property deeds. Replacing these documents is a hassle if lost, and the fallout is far worse if they leak.

**Financial records:** Bank account numbers, insurance policies, investment statements and tax documents. Their value speaks for itself.

**Medical records:** Physical exam reports, medical histories, prescriptions and mental health assessments. You would not want insurers or employers to view these. As the revised Food Safety Law fuels the development of digital health records, managing personal medical data will grow ever more important — but until then, you must protect it yourself.

**Passwords and keys:** Account passwords, crypto wallet seed phrases and API keys. These are the keys to your digital life and should never sit in plaintext on your phone.

**Personal files:** Journals, letters, private records meant for your eyes only. Everyone deserves a fully private space.

## A Small Thought Experiment

Imagine two options:
A. Print all sensitive files on your phone, seal them in an envelope, and ask an acquaintance you barely know to keep it safe.
B. Encrypt those files and store them in DoraBox.

You would almost certainly pick B. Handing copies of your ID to a stranger sounds absurd.

Yet storing ID photos in your phone gallery is essentially doing the same thing. You hand your data over to phone manufacturers, operating system vendors and any app that may access your storage. They are that “acquaintance you barely know”.

Put simply, DoraBox lets **your most important data belong only to you.**

Not built on trust. Built on encryption.
