## A Question: When Did You Last Audit Your “Storage”?

I am not asking about remaining space on your phone.

I mean those files you keep: scanned contracts, ID photos, your child’s birth certificate, your parents’ medical reports, last year’s tax records. Where are they right now?

You probably know they sit somewhere in a cloud drive. But which one? What encryption standard is used? How many copies exist? Which data center hosts them? Can you retrieve them anytime you need?

Chances are you do not know.

This is the awkward reality of personal storage in 2026:
**The whole world is going crazy over storage, yet almost no one offers a serious answer for individual users.**

Enterprise storage falls under the CIO’s remit. Datacenter storage is managed by operation teams. AI model storage is handled by algorithm engineers.

Your storage? It falls on you — and you are almost certainly not a storage specialist.

## “Storage” and “Stashing Files” Are Not the Same Thing

Let me spell this out plainly.

Dropping a contract into iCloud or a cloud disk is not storage. It is merely stashing.

**True storage comes with a management framework.**
Enterprise-grade storage includes access controls, permission tiering, encryption protection, versioning, disaster recovery, and compliance auditing.

Stashing has none of that. You upload a file and cross your fingers it will not vanish, leak, or get fed into AI training by the platform.

The 2026 outlook speaks for itself: the global data privacy protection market is projected to expand from 24.37 billion yuan to 52.73 billion yuan, with a CAGR of 11.5%. Countries keep rolling out personal data protection regulations. MIIT has already issued notices for 60 batches of risky apps violating user rights this year.

Still, most ordinary users manage their data the same way they did a decade ago — upload it and forget it.

## Memory Chips Jumped 15% — Has the Value of Your Data Gone Up?

Wall Street priced in that premium for memory chips because the market believes:
**Data is the oil of the new age, and storage is the barrel holding that oil.**

But what is your own data worth?

IBM’s 2026 report offers a benchmark: the average global cost of a data breach stands at $4.99 million, and the cost per leaked personal record hits $188.

$188 per record, roughly 1,350 RMB.

How many personal records live on your phone? ID numbers, mobile numbers, bank details, home addresses, workplace info, emails, social security IDs, driver’s license numbers… conservatively 30 records.

30 × 1,350 = 40,500 RMB.

That does not count your contracts, financial statements, medical records, and private chat logs. The digital assets of an average professional can easily exceed 100,000 RMB.

So what does the memory chip rally mean for you? A lot — **you spent tens of thousands on phones and computers holding over 100k worth of your data, yet rely on free, unmanaged stashing services.**

It is like buying a Mercedes and leaving it unlocked by the curb.
