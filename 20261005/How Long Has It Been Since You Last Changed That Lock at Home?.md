# How Long Has It Been Since You Last Changed That Lock at Home?

During the National Day holiday, my mom asked me to help organize the photo album on her phone.

Halfway through sorting, she suddenly grew anxious and asked me: “Uh… can other people see the ID photos I saved on my phone?”

I told her if stored locally on the phone, generally no.

She followed up: “What if I upload them to a cloud drive? Last time your dad wanted me to send him my medical report, I shared it using a cloud disk link.”

I paused. It dawned on me that she might have uploaded photos of ID cards, household registration booklets, bank cards, and medical reports for the whole family, all mixed into a folder named “Important Documents” on some cloud storage service whose name she couldn’t recall.

Worse still, the shared link had no password protection and no expiration date.

## The “Drawer” in the Digital Age

Every household has a drawer.

When we were kids, it held diaries and love letters — fitted with a lock, its key hidden inside a pencil case. As adults, it stores property deeds, household registration papers and insurance policies, tucked deep inside a wardrobe or deposited in a safe deposit box at the bank.

Privacy protection in the physical world follows a simple logic: valuables → placed in a container → locked up → you hold the key.

In the digital age, this logic has broken down. Your “valuables” have turned into photos, documents, passwords and chat records. Scattered across phones, computers, cloud drives, email accounts and various apps, they may seem secure, yet they can be easier to access than a parcel locker outside your front door.

By 2026, the average person worldwide owns more than 120 online accounts, with personal data stored on various services totaling 3.6 terabytes per individual on average. Less than 5% of this data is marked by users as “sensitive” or “important” — yet it is this 5% that carries the most severe consequences if leaked.

## DoraBox: The Safe in the Digital World

DoraBox is built for that 5%.

It is not a cloud drive, note-taking app, or photo backup tool. It is an encrypted storage container. Think of it as a locked digital drawer built exclusively for the things you never want others to see.

What exactly can DoraBox do?
**Document Management:** Scan or photograph ID cards, passports, driver’s licenses, household registers, property deeds and more, then save them in DoraBox. Files get encrypted automatically. When you need access, unlock locally to view them. At all other times, they exist only as indecipherable ciphertext.

**Medical Records:** Medical checkup reports, medical histories, prescriptions and genetic test results. This data is far more sensitive than most people realize. A leaked genetic dataset could affect your insurance premiums for decades to come. DoraBox ensures these files are only opened when you choose to access them.

**Financial Information:** Bank statements, payslips, tax records and investment screenshots. No platform backend needs to casually peek into your financial status.

**Private Journals and Footage:** Some people keep diaries or record videos on their phones, content even more private than chat messages. DoraBox offers a local encrypted space secured by biometric authentication (fingerprint / facial recognition), so only you can gain entry.

## How Is It Different from DoraCloud?

If DoraCloud is your digital warehouse — able to hold everything from small to large files while balancing security and convenience — then DoraBox is the innermost safe inside that warehouse: compact, but built to the highest protection standard.

DoraCloud works well for everyday files, work documents, videos and images. DoraBox is reserved for content that “must never fall into other people’s hands.” The two complement each other rather than replacing one another.

To draw an analogy: you would not store your property deed in the same drawer as takeout menus. DoraCloud is your filing cabinet; DoraBox is your safe.

## Why Encryption Should Not Be Complicated

Many people feel overwhelmed at the mere mention of “encryption”: key management, password strength, two-factor authentication… it sounds like work reserved for the IT department.

DoraBox’s design principle: **Make encryption as simple as locking a drawer.**

- Open the app and unlock via face ID or fingerprint;
- Drag and drop files in for automatic encryption;
- You can view files anytime once stored;
- No one but you can access them.

No complicated configurations, no lengthy passphrases to memorize, no anxiety over password recovery. (That said, you still need to safely store your recovery mnemonic phrase — it serves as the final security gate.)
