# Is There a Folder on Your Phone That Even You Dare Not Open?

It’s 1:30 a.m. I get back to my hotel after overtime and unzip my suitcase to sort my belongings.

At the bottom, a kraft-paper envelope catches my eye. Inside are copies of the property deed, my ID card, and a handwritten note with a bank account number and password — stuff my mum pressed on me last time we met.

“Keep this, just in case,” she said, her tone almost as if she were making final arrangements.

I gave a bitter smile. I could lock those paper documents in my suitcase, but what about their digital versions? Photos of my ID front and back, scanned driver’s license, PDF medical reports, and that memo with all my passwords — they sit right now in my phone storage, mixed with food delivery receipts and short-video cache files.

## 37 Minutes After the Phone Went Missing

Last summer, a colleague of mine had his phone stolen while on a business trip.

He reported the theft to police at the 10-minute mark, remotely locked the device at 15 minutes, and changed every password he could think of by minute 20. Yet at minute 37, a bank alert popped up on his phone: someone had logged into his online banking using his SMS verification code and transferred out a sum of money.

Though the phone was locked, the thief had intercepted the SMS verification code before the remote lock kicked in. Later, it was confirmed that ID photos and the password memo stored locally on the phone had been fully extracted.

After that incident, I made a decision: delete all sensitive files from my phone album and notes, and move them to a dedicated secure place.

The question was: where is secure enough?

## The Logic of a Safe

DoraBox is built on a straightforward idea: take the data on your phone that you “cannot afford to lose, dare not leave exposed, yet must keep” and put it inside a genuine digital safe.

It is no fancy cloud sync tool, but an app focused on encrypted storage for private data. It has one core purpose: **securely store your sensitive files after encryption so only you can access them.**

Behind the word “encryption”, DoraBox stands out with several key design choices:

**Military-grade encryption standard.** It adopts the AES-256 encryption algorithm, widely used by financial institutions and military bodies worldwide. Without the encryption key, brute-force cracking of ciphertext is not feasible with current computing power.

**Zero-knowledge architecture.** DoraBox’s servers never know your encryption key. That means even DoraBox staff cannot open your safe. This is not merely a promise — it is baked into the technical architecture. Your key is generated and stored locally on your device and never uploaded to any server.

**Biometric unlocking.** Fingerprint and facial recognition serve as quick access methods. Even your biometric data stays on your local device and is never sent to third-party servers.

**Decoy vault.** This feature is rather clever. You can set a decoy password. Entering it opens a fake safe filled with trivial files. In the unlikely event someone forces you to unlock your phone (such as during a robbery), you can open the decoy vault to hand over harmless content.

## What Should Go Inside the Safe?

Everyone’s list differs, but these categories are generally undisputed:

- **Identity documents**: Scans of ID cards, passports, driver’s licenses and property deeds. Replacing them after loss is troublesome, and leakage is far worse.
- **Financial records**: Bank account details, insurance policies, investment records and tax documents.
- **Medical files**: Health check reports, medical histories and prescriptions. Information you would not want insurers or employers to see.
- **Passwords & keys**: Account passwords, crypto wallet seed phrases, API keys and more.
- **Personal records**: Diaries, letters and private notes meant for no one else’s eyes.

This is not about “hiding things”. It is about giving your most important digital information the proper place it deserves in the digital age.

## An Incident During the National Day Holiday

As I was writing this article, someone shared a news story in our group chat: a newly hired delivery worker had lost 10 iPhones. Some commenters said “good thing the phones are locked”, while others argued “locks don’t help; data can be extracted anyway”.

Both views hold partial truth, yet neither is fully correct. The crux is whether the data stored on those phones was kept in plaintext or encrypted.

If photos, files and notes are stored in plaintext on the phone, the lock screen password can stop ordinary people, but not professional extraction tools. But if your sensitive data lives in an encrypted environment like DoraBox from the start, your most valuable information remains protected even if your phone is lost or cracked.

>
> **The best way to protect privacy is not to stop thieves from unlocking your phone, but to ensure they see nothing even if they do.**

## That Kraft-Paper Envelope

Back to the opening story. I took photos of the documents in the envelope my mum gave me and stored them in DoraBox, then mailed the physical papers back to my hometown.

The digital copies sit encrypted inside the safe, accessible only to me. The physical originals rest quietly in a drawer back home.

Peace of mind on both fronts.

This is what privacy ought to feel like. Not secrecy or concealment, but knowing your important information is safely stored — so you can set your worries aside and get on with your life.
