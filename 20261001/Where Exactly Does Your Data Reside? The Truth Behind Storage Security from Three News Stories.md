# Where Exactly Does Your Data Reside? The Truth Behind Storage Security from Three News Stories

## News 1: 31% — A New "Top Cause"

Verizon’s 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR) documents a historic shift: **31% of data breaches stem from software vulnerabilities**, overtaking stolen passwords for the first time to become attackers’ primary route into systems.

In other words, no matter how complex your password is, your data can still be exposed if the platform hosting it has vulnerabilities. Passwords guard the "door", while exploits cut straight through the "walls".

For everyday users, this paints a stark reality: **where you store your files may matter more than what password you set.**

## News 2: Your Hardware Information Quietly Sent Overseas

In recent enforcement cases published by the Cyberspace Administration of China, a Shanghai tech firm was found guilty of misconduct. Starting in 2022, its Windows application persistently collected users’ host configurations, usage durations and system information beyond permitted scope. It transmitted this data, alongside users’ names, phone numbers, email addresses, birthdays, passwords and other details, to offshore data centers.

This is no isolated incident. Out of sight, many "free" cloud storage services are doing the same thing. You may think you are merely storing a file, yet your usage habits, access frequency, device information and even metadata embedded within your files are being collected and analyzed.

**Free services often come with the highest cost — and the bill is charged to your privacy.**

## News 3: Google Apologized, but the Data Was Already Leaked

This September, Google issued a public apology in South Korea over allegations that it exposed personal information of victims of digital sex crimes. The root cause: Google directly shared victims’ deletion requests with an overseas research project website it collaborated with, resulting in the disclosure of sensitive details including victims’ names and workplaces.

Note that this was not a hack by cybercriminals. Instead, **flaws existed within the platform’s own data handling workflows.** Victims reached out seeking protection, yet the platform’s operations inflicted greater harm.

This reveals a deeper issue: even when a platform intends to protect you, its complex internal workflows, cross-border data transfers and third-party partnerships can create leakage points at every stage.

## One Shared Takeaway from the Three Stories

It is not that passwords are too weak or hackers are too skilled. The core truth is: **when your data sits on someone else’s servers, your control over it drops close to zero.**

You may choose to trust a platform, but trust does not equal security. Platforms can suffer breaches, develop vulnerabilities, revise policies, get acquired or shut down. In any of these scenarios, your data may suffer consequences.

This is not fear-mongering. It is the landscape of data security in 2026.

## A Practical Checklist to Judge Data Security

How to tell whether your data storage is secure? Ask yourself three questions:
**First: If this platform shuts down tomorrow, will I still have my data?**
Data counts as yours only if it can be exported.

**Second: If this platform suffers an attack, will my sensitive files leak?**
Only end-to-end encryption can answer "no".

**Third: If this platform changes its privacy policy, can I stop it from accessing my files?**
If you do not hold the keys, the answer will always be "no".
