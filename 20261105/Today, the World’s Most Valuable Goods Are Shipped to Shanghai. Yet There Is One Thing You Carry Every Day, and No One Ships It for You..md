The 9th China International Import Expo opens today. The President of Gabon, the Chancellor of Austria, the Vice President of Cuba, and political and business leaders from dozens of countries have gathered along the Huangpu River, centering their discussions on one core theme — **how goods and services can cross borders more freely.**

Meanwhile, tucked away on your phone, there is a file — perhaps an ID photo you took last month, a medical checkup report from last year, or a scanned copy of a contract you just signed. It sits quietly on a server somewhere you cannot name.

No customs declaration. No insurance. No tracking.

**The CIIE debates how to move goods safely across borders. For your files, no one can even answer where their borders lie.**

USD 188, roughly ¥1,350.

Take out your phone right now and count: ID number, phone number, bank card details, residential address, employer, email… You can easily list more than 20 items in 30 seconds.

**20 × 1350 = ¥27,000.**

And that only accounts for basic identifiers. Add contracts, financial records, medical data, chat logs — the digital assets of an ordinary office worker can easily be valued at over one hundred thousand yuan.

Here is the question: Would you leave ¥100,000 in cash in an unlocked drawer?

## A Classification Problem Most People Overlook

I have watched how people around me use cloud drives.

Nine out of ten do the same thing: dump everything into one folder. Photos, videos, contracts, ID scans, payslips, children’s report cards, travel itineraries — all mixed together.

It is the equivalent of putting your property deed, passport and bank card into the same cardboard box as yesterday’s takeout receipt, then handing the box over to a stranger for safekeeping.

**The problem is not where you store things. It is the lack of separation.**

Your travel snapshots and bank statements have vastly different security requirements. Losing the photos may upset you for an afternoon; a leak of your bank records can leave you targeted by precision phishing for a full year. Still, you store them in the same place, under the same level of protection.

That leaves only two bad options:

- Encrypt everything: too slow and cumbersome. You will turn it off by day three.
- Encrypt nothing: convenient, but your data is exposed in plaintext.

This forced binary dilemma is what almost everyone faces.

## The Lesson from the CIIE

What is the essence of the Import Expo?

**Segmented, tiered management.**

Medical devices go through medical approval channels, food through food safety checks, vehicles through 3C certification, artworks through cultural import procedures. Every category of goods has its own protection tier, oversight rules and clearance workflow.

No one ships a crate of vaccines in the same container as instant noodles, under identical temperature controls.

But your files get exactly that treatment: all items lumped into one folder, protected the same way.

Why this design?

A plain truth: **If security is too inconvenient, people will bypass it.**

Encrypting every single photo? Sync slows down, operations grow tedious. Sooner or later you will give up — and your ID photos lose protection along with everything else.

DoraCloud’s logic: keep everyday files fast and fluid, while applying maximum safeguards to sensitive data. **Each category stays in its place, without disrupting the other.**

Just like the expo halls: medical devices in the medical pavilion, food in the food pavilion, cars in the auto pavilion. It is not overcomplicating things. It is recognizing that **different assets deserve different protection.**

## “Who Holds the Key?” — The One Takeaway to Remember

If you retain only one cybersecurity lesson for life, let it be this:

**When it comes to encryption, the critical question is not whether something is encrypted — but who holds the decryption key.**

Popular cloud storage services do offer encryption. But their providers hold the keys. That means:

- Provider staff can technically view your files
- The provider’s AI systems may analyze your content to refine recommendations
- The provider can decrypt and hand over data when served with legal orders
- If the provider suffers a breach, hackers may steal both ciphertext and keys together

DoraCloud works differently. Files are encrypted on your device, and keys remain on your device only. Servers hold nothing but ciphertext.

**This is not about trusting DoraCloud not to peek. It is that DoraCloud is technically incapable of peeking.**

Cryptography relies not on trust, but on math.
