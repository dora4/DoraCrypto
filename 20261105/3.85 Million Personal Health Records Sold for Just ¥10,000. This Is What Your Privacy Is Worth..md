## Why Are They So Cheap?

You may feel furious. But a more important question to ask is: **why so cheap?**

The answer is harsh: **your data was not encrypted.**

Cybersecurity has a fundamental distinction: plaintext data and ciphertext data.

Plaintext data — readable names, phone numbers, medical records — carries a clear black-market price tag. It can be used directly for targeted scams, identity theft, and insurance fraud.

Ciphertext data — a stream of unintelligible bytes — is **nearly worthless on the black market**. Buyers cannot tell what is inside, nor how to unlock it. It is like buying a book written in an unknown language, filled with nothing but garbled characters.

The fact that 3.85 million records sold for barely ¥10,000 proves the data was **plaintext or near-plaintext**. Attackers could use it immediately with no decryption required.

What if your medical records had been encrypted?

Attackers would only seize meaningless noise. Noise cannot be sold. Noise has no value. Noise protects you.

That is encryption’s core value: **it does not merely make data harder to steal — it makes data useless once stolen.**

## But Encryption ≠ Security — A Hidden Pitfall Most People Miss

At this point you may think: My cloud drive offers encryption too; I set a password.

Let us address a critical question: **Who holds the encryption key?**

Most cloud storage and file managers work like this: your files are encrypted, but the decryption keys live on the provider’s servers.

What does that mean?

- Provider staff or insiders can decrypt your files using those keys
- If the provider’s systems are breached, hackers may steal both ciphertext and keys together
- When compelled by authorities, the provider can decrypt and hand over your data
- The provider’s AI systems may train on your data in the background

**This is not true encryption. It is a lock with the key hanging right on the doorknob.**

Genuine encryption means the key belongs only to you. Not mostly you. Not by default you. **Only you.**
