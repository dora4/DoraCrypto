**It shifts the paradigm from "AI-enabled software" to "agent-reconstructed software."**

Older AI policies follow this logic: add AI capabilities to existing software. Put plainly: your app remains your app; it just gets smarter.

But Beijing’s new guidance charts a different path:
**It encourages innovators to rebuild underlying architectures and interaction paradigms around agents.**

This is not renovating a house. It is tearing it down and rebuilding from scratch.

An Agent is no longer just a chat window embedded inside an app. It becomes a digital proxy capable of autonomous decision-making, independent execution and cross-system orchestration. It can send emails for you, schedule meetings, organize documents and process approvals. It develops its own judgment, and in certain scenarios, it can even **make decisions on your behalf**.

Here comes the critical question:
**When your agent reads, organizes and distributes your files — who is actually viewing them?**

## A Question You May Not Have Considered

Picture this scenario:
You have an AI agent managing your cloud storage. It automatically sorts newly taken photos into a "Family" folder, files scanned contracts under "Work", and recognizes invoices before archiving them in "Finance".

It is incredibly convenient.

But to do all this, the agent must **read the content inside every single file**. Otherwise, how could it tell whether an image is a family snapshot or a contract? How could it distinguish a PDF invoice from a resume?

**That means every file you own is being "seen" by an AI system.**

Under most mainstream cloud storage services, this AI runs on cloud computing resources. In other words, your files are not only held by the storage provider at rest; their contents are parsed and scanned one by one by AI for semantic understanding.

This is not DoraCloud pointing fingers at any competitor. It is a structural reality:
**The more capable an agent becomes, the more data it needs to see. And the blurrier your privacy boundary grows.**

## The Antinomy of the Agent Era

Philosophically, an antinomy describes two propositions that are each valid on their own yet contradict one another.
Data governance in the agent age faces exactly this antinomy:

**Proposition A: Agents need access to your data to function.**
This is a technical fact. Without access to your calendar, it cannot arrange meetings. Without reading your emails, it cannot draft replies. Without seeing your files, it cannot sort and organize them.

**Proposition B: Your data must be protected and cannot be exposed indiscriminately.**
This is equally true. Your contracts should not feed ad-training models. Your medical records should not inform insurance pricing. Your ID photos should never become raw material for any system.

Both statements hold true — yet they conflict.

Most tech companies prioritize Proposition A: grant agents broad data access, then reassure users with privacy policies promising safety.
DoraCloud aims to satisfy both A and B at the same time.

## Partitioning: A Seemingly Simple Yet Highly Effective Solution

DoraCloud’s core design principle can be summed up in one word: **separation**.

It splits your files into two zones:
**General File Zone** — photos, videos, documents, slides and everyday office files. These sync rapidly across devices and open instantly. Your AI agent can operate freely here: classification, search, sorting and recommendations work without restriction for maximum convenience.

**Private Data Zone** — ID documents, contracts, financial records, medical reports and password notes. This zone uses end-to-end encryption. Files are encrypted locally on your device before upload. Encryption keys stay exclusively with you. The server only stores ciphertext.

The two zones are fully isolated.
**AI agents cannot see files inside the Private Data Zone.**
This is not an optional restriction, nor a mere promise. It is technically impossible: files are encrypted into ciphertext before upload, and decryption keys never leave your devices.

You might ask: then the AI cannot organize files in the encrypted zone?
Precisely. That is a deliberate tradeoff built into DoraCloud.
**Some things should not be sorted. They should be locked away.**

Your ID scan does not need AI tagging. Your banking contract does not need AI labels. Your medical checkup report does not need AI suggesting related content.
These files only have one requirement: **only you can open them.**

## Why Partitioning Is Smarter Than Full-File Encryption

Some may ask: why not encrypt everything once and for all?

Cybersecurity has a well-known answer to this:
**If security is too cumbersome, users will bypass it.**

Behavioral security research shows that when encryption is forced on all files, over 70% of users disable it within two weeks. The reasons are straightforward: slower sync, clunky operations, laggy mobile performance — people simply give up.

The end result? Even sensitive files that should have been protected lose their safeguards.

That is why DoraCloud chooses partitioning over universal encryption. Daily files stay fast and fluid; sensitive assets receive top-tier protection. Each category serves its purpose without interfering with the other.

Beijing’s agent policy similarly expands support beyond software vendors to cover the whole industrial chain: foundation models, computing power, frameworks, applications and end devices each have distinct roles. This complexity exists for a reason: **different assets call for different strategies.**

The same applies to your files.
