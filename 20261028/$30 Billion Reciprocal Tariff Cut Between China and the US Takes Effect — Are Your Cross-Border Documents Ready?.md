## "A Cloud Drive Password" and "An Encrypted File" Are Two Separate Things

Let’s clear up this misconception.

**Scenario A:** You upload a scanned contract to a mainstream cloud drive. Your login password secures your account, but the file itself is stored as plaintext on the provider’s servers. In theory, the provider’s engineers, operation staff and AI review systems can all access the file content.

**Scenario B:** You encrypt the same contract locally before uploading it. Only an indecipherable ciphertext is saved on the cloud server. The decryption key stays solely on your device. Even if the server suffers a breach, is subjected to inspection, or accessed by insiders, all anyone sees is meaningless garbled code.

Following the rollout of the China-US trade framework, cross-border files are growing both in volume and sensitivity. Are your contracts, quotations, technical specifications and client data sitting in Scenario A — or Scenario B?

This is not a minor technical detail. It is the **foundation of your commercial security.**
