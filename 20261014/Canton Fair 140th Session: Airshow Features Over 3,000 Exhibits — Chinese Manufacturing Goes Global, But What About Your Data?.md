## The "Invisible Channel" for Data Going Overseas

You may think “data going overseas” is only a corporate issue and has nothing to do with you.

Consider these scenarios: you store work documents on an international cloud drive hosted in a data center in Virginia, US. You write a private journal in a note-taking app whose data syncs to servers abroad. You edit a photo with an image editing tool, and the original image is uploaded for “AI training and optimization.”

These activities happen every single day, yet you are never informed.

In 2026, global data breaches continue to grow in scale. South Korea’s five major commercial banks suffered synchronized AI-powered hacker attacks in the first half of the year. Around 25,000 customers of Shinhan Bank had their names, incomes and loan limits stolen. It took a full 25 hours for the breach to be detected after the attack started. The US Pentagon’s personnel data system was compromised, exposing personal information of 2.8 million service members, and some of the data was even unencrypted.

This is not a problem confined to one country. It is a systemic global risk — **the centralized architecture where data is stored in bulk inherently makes it the top target for attackers.**

## The "Sovereignty" Issue of Cloud Storage

Traditional cloud drives, whether run by large or small vendors, essentially do the same thing: collect your files onto their servers and grant you convenient “anytime access.”

The benefits of this model are obvious: ease of use, cross-device synchronization and low costs. Yet its drawbacks are equally prominent:

**You have no physical control over your own data.** Your files sit on someone else’s hard drives. The provider can view and analyze them, and hand them over under the pretext of “complying with legal requirements.” You probably never read the user agreement you signed, but it typically grants the service provider broad rights to access your data.

**Your private and regular files share the same security policy.** Your family photos and bank statements reside in the same cloud storage, protected by the same password. However, these two types of data have vastly different security needs. A leak of photos may lead to embarrassment; a leak of financial records can trigger a disaster.

**You cannot audit the service provider’s security capabilities.** It claims to implement encryption, backups and security audits — yet you have no way to verify these claims. You can only choose to “trust.”

In 2026, “trust” alone is no longer sufficient.

## DoraCloud: Putting Control Back in Users’ Hands

DoraCloud aims to solve a straightforward problem: **let you retain control of your files, both physically and logically.**

It is not just “another cloud drive.” It is a storage product redesigned from the ground up at the architectural level, with three core differentiators:

**Client-side encryption; keys remain in your possession.** Your files are encrypted locally on your device before upload. Data is transmitted as ciphertext and stays encrypted once it reaches the server. DoraCloud’s servers only store encrypted data and hold no decryption keys. Even if the server is breached, attackers will only get unreadable garbled text.

**Decentralized storage architecture.** After sharding and encryption, files are distributed across multiple nodes. No single node holds a complete copy of your file. This eliminates the single point of failure where breaching one location can expose all your data.

**Partitioned management for sensitive and regular data.** DoraCloud comes with a built-in partitioning system. Your work documents, travel photos and everyday files can be placed in the regular zone for easy synchronization and sharing. Sensitive materials such as ID scans, financial records and private diagonals go to the privacy zone with independent encryption rules and access controls. Much like your home with ordinary drawers and a safe: different items are stored separately.
