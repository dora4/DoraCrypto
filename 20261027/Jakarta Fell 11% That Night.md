# Jakarta Fell 11% That Night

Lao Zhou called me while I was having dinner.

"Did you see the news? MSCI is going to kick Indonesia out of its global index." His voice was tight, not the panicked kind — that peculiar restrained tension investors get when facing a black swan event.

I pulled up my market terminal and took a glance. The Jakarta Composite Index plunged more than 11% intraday, triggering a circuit breaker.

"What’s that got to do with me?" I asked.

Lao Zhou fell silent for two seconds. "Do you remember where you stored the seed phrases for your crypto wallet?"

I set down my chopsticks.

## One Retail Investor’s Digital Fortune

Lao Zhou is my college roommate, now a researcher at a private equity fund. He is not the type of retail trader chasing rallies and cutting losses; his portfolio focuses on long-term value investing. But he has a habit — or rather, a flaw: he keeps loads of critical data on his phone.

He took screenshots of his crypto wallet seed phrases and saved them in his photo album. Exchange API Keys were typed into his notes app. PDFs of early investment agreements sat in WeChat File Transfer Assistant. Screenshots of bank card numbers and passwords were tucked into a photo folder he thought was well hidden.

"This isn’t investing; your data is completely exposed," I told him many times.

"I know, but I keep thinking — my phone has a lock screen passcode, the album has a hidden folder feature, nobody’s going to dig through my notes. It should be fine."

*Should be.*

That single word is the most dangerous phrase in data security.

## When That 11% Becomes Your 11%

On the surface, MSCI’s consideration to remove Indonesia from its global index was a capital market event. Yet its ripple effects were real: massive capital outflows from emerging markets, violent currency swings, forcing investors to rebalance positions in an extremely tight window.

At moments like this, you need to log into trading platforms quickly, check holdings and execute orders. If you lose your crypto wallet seed phrase — not because the market crashed, but because something went wrong with your phone — you get locked out right when action matters most.

Flip it around: what if someone gets hold of your seed phrase at this moment? Amid wild market volatility, your wallet could be drained before you even react.

This is no alarmist talk. In Q3 this year, global security losses in the crypto sector hit $1.26 billion, with a single $387 million heist on Bitget. Personal losses often never even make the statistics. Phones get lost, picked up, data extracted, wallets emptied — these incidents happen every single day, they just rarely make headlines.

## The Story of a Vault

I told Lao Zhou a story later.

A friend’s phone was stolen last month. He remotely locked the device at the 12th minute and changed all passwords by the 20th minute. But photos of his ID card, scanned driver’s license, and the notes file storing all his passwords were fully exported in those 12 minutes before he locked the phone.

He spent three months reissuing various documents, and far more time untangling cascading issues triggered by the password leak.

That’s when I started using a tool called DoraBox. It is not an ordinary file manager; it is an **encrypted safe built exclusively for sensitive data**.

Its core logic is straightforward: move the items on your phone that “must not be lost, must not leak, yet need to be stored digitally” out of photo albums, notes and file explorers, into an independent space protected by AES‑256 encryption.

AES‑256 is the encryption standard widely adopted by financial institutions and military bodies. Without the encryption key, brute-forcing encrypted files is infeasible under current computing power.

More importantly, it adopts a **zero-knowledge architecture**. Encryption keys are generated and stored locally only on your own device. DoraBox’s servers never know your keys. That means even DoraBox’s own staff cannot open your vault. This is not a marketing promise; it is baked into the technical architecture.

## Lao Zhou’s Choice

I did not lecture him over the phone. I only asked one question:

“How many photos of your seed phrases do you have on your phone?”

He paused. “Three. For two wallets.”

“If your phone disappeared right now, what could you possibly do amid that 11% market crash?”

“…Nothing.”

The Jakarta market closed roughly 8% lower that night. Not the most brutal crash, but enough to cost many investors sleep.

Early the next morning, Lao Zhou messaged me: “I installed that vault and moved all my seed phrases and passwords into it.”

He added a line: “The decoy entry feature is pretty neat.”

Decoy entry is one of DoraBox’s designs. You can set a fake password that unlocks a dummy vault filled with trivial files, which looks indistinguishable from the real vault. If someone coerces you to unlock your phone, you can open the decoy vault for them, while your real vault stays untouched.

## Markets Bounce Back

I did not keep tracking what happened next to Jakarta’s stock market. Volatility is inherent to financial markets. Today it drops 11%, tomorrow it may rebound 5%, and the next day brings another black swan. MSCI’s reclassification will either go through or be put on hold; Indonesia’s economic fundamentals will not shift because of an index decision.

But one thing is certain: **your digital assets will not become safer just because the market rebounds.**

A seed phrase saved in your phone album is exposed. Passwords written in notes are fragile. ID photos mixed with selfies can be extracted easily.

None of this has anything to do with market ups and downs. It all boils down to one question: have you given your most critical assets a secure home?

That is exactly what DoraBox solves. It does not help you earn money or guide your investments. It simply locks away your digital belongings. Whether it is a bull or bear market, circuit breaker or sharp rally, your keys remain firmly in your hands.

That is perhaps the plainest definition of “security.”
