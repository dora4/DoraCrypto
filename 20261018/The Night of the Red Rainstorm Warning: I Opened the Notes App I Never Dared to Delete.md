# The Night of the Red Rainstorm Warning: I Opened the Notes App I Never Dared to Delete

Outside the window, wind rattled the clothes hanger.

My phone screen lit up with three simultaneous alerts: an orange rainstorm warning, a yellow gale warning, and a yellow lightning warning. Conditions were far worse back home in Hunan — Changde had issued a red rainstorm warning. My mom sent a voice message saying water was flooding the balcony, telling me not to worry.

How could I not worry?

But I don’t want to talk about the weather today. I want to talk about what hit me that stormy night.

## The Thirty Seconds Before My Phone Got Waterlogged

Last summer, Changsha also saw a heavy rainstorm. I’d been running errands all day, and the downpour picked up suddenly on my way home. My umbrella was useless against it. I arrived soaked head to toe, pulled my phone out of my pocket, and saw a layer of mist covering the screen.

The phone still worked. Yet in those thirty seconds — between noticing it was wet and confirming it still functioned — my first thought wasn’t *Oh no, I need a new phone*. It was *What about everything stored on this phone?*

What kind of things?
Photos of my ID card and scanned driver’s license saved in the photo album.
All sorts of passwords jotted down in Notes.
Screenshots of medical checkup reports saved in WeChat Favorites.
PDF contracts downloaded in the file manager.

These files share one trait: **Losing them would cost ten thousand times more than replacing the phone itself.**

A phone can be replaced. Data cannot.

## A Habit Many Have, Yet Few Will Admit

Do you keep these things on your phone too?

- Front-and-back photos of your ID card (snapped for administrative tasks and forgotten about)
- Screenshots of bank card numbers (saved in photos so you wouldn’t have to memorize them)
- Notes filled with account passwords written in your own private shorthand
- Photos of medical reports and medical records (after losing the paper copies from the hospital)
- Pictures of property deeds and rental contracts (taken during the last move)

These files sit on your phone, mingled with food delivery screenshots, short-video cache files, and memes.

Your phone lock screen password keeps ordinary people out. But it cannot stand up to professional data recovery tools, malicious software, or repair shop staff who “kindly” offer to back up your data while fixing your phone.

Worse still, you don’t even need to be “hacked.” If your phone gets lost, water-damaged or smashed, your data vanishes along with it.

## What If There Was a Place Built Specifically for These Files?

DoraBox exists to answer one question: **Where should you store your most sensitive files on your phone?**

It is not a photo album, not a notes app, not a file manager. It is a digital safe deposit box. You put your most critical items inside, and it encrypts and guards them — accessible only to you.

Here is what it does:
**Encryption.** It uses the AES-256 standard, the same grade adopted by banks. Once your files are stored inside, they turn into ciphertext. Without your key, anyone opening it will only see meaningless strings of characters.

**Zero-knowledge architecture.** DoraBox itself never knows your key. Your key is generated locally on your device and never uploaded to any server. That means even if DoraBox’s servers are breached, attackers will only get a locked safe — with no key to open it.

**Biometric authentication.** Unlock with fingerprint or face ID for easy daily use. Your biometric data stays locally on your phone and never travels to any server.

**Decoy vault.** This clever feature lets you set a fake password. Entering it opens a decoy box holding trivial, unimportant files. In extreme scenarios where someone forces you to unlock your device, you can show them the decoy vault to satisfy their demand.
