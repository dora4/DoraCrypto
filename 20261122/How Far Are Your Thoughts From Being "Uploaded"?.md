# How Far Are Your Thoughts From Being "Uploaded"?

## — Written on the Closing Day of the 2026 Brain-Computer Interface Conference

Yet as an ordinary person reading these updates, instead of rejoicing over technological leaps, I’m left with an unsettling question:
**What if one day your thoughts, emotions and even subconscious impulses can be read by a device — where will that data be stored?**

## From Data You Upload to Data Collected From You

When we talk about data privacy today, we mostly refer to content you actively upload:
Photos, documents, chat logs, bank statements, medical reports. You generate this data, and in theory you get to decide where it is kept and who you share it with.

But technology is pushing data toward passive generation.

Smart bands track your heart rate and sleep patterns. Your phone logs location trails and usage habits. Voice assistants listen for wake words. Recommendation algorithms infer your preferences, moods and decision-making tendencies.

You did not “upload” this data. It is **collected** by devices. You have almost no control over its creation.

Brain-computer interfaces take this trend to its extreme.

BeiNao No.1 is designed to help paralyzed patients regain motor function and enable people with speech loss to communicate again. This is undeniably extraordinary work. Still, the fundamental nature of brain-computer interface technology is this:
**It directly reads neural signals from the brain, converts them into digital data, then processes, transmits and stores that data.**

Neural signals are not fingerprints, facial scans or passwords. They are the underlying bioelectrical signals inside your brain — the physical carrier of your thoughts, feelings and intentions.

Once this data is captured: Which servers will it pass through? How many copies will be made? Who will have access to read it?

When your photos travel to cloud storage today, you at least have some sense of where they go. When “brain data” travels in the future, you may never even know it is happening.

## Three Leaps in Privacy: From My Files to My Mind

If we rank data by how intimately tied it is to you, a clear hierarchy emerges:

**Tier 1: Utility Data**
Work documents and project files on your phone. This data relates to you but is not an extension of you. Losing it is inconvenient, not catastrophic.

**Tier 2: Identity Data**
ID photos, bank statements, medical records, scanned contracts. This data is directly linked to your legal identity and financial status. A leak can lead to impersonation, fraud and targeted phishing.

**Tier 3: Biometric Data**
Fingerprints, facial features, iris scans, voiceprints, DNA. This data *is* you. Once exposed, it cannot be replaced. You cannot get a new face or a fresh set of fingerprints.

**Tier 4: Neural Data**
Brainwaves, neural signals, patterns of thought. This data is not just you — it captures the unspoken you: emotions, leanings and intentions you may not even recognize in yourself.

The first three tiers already circulate widely on cloud platforms today. Tier four is moving out of laboratories and toward commercial products.

Each tier carries exponentially greater privacy risk than the last. Yet most people treat neural data the same way they treat ordinary documents:
**“Just store it in the cloud.”**

## The Question Is Not Whether the Cloud Is Secure — But Whether You Control Your Data

Two opposing views always surface in privacy discussions.

One camp says: “Big tech clouds are secure, far more reliable than your own hard drive.”

The other says: “Don’t use the cloud at all; keep everything local.”

Both points hold some truth, yet both miss the core issue.

Major cloud providers maintain robust security. So did TSMC — yet on November 12, 46,000 employee records were leaked after a supplier breach. London’s transport authority also had strong safeguards, yet 265,000 Oyster Card users’ information was stolen by hackers this month.

**The real problem is not whether the cloud is secure enough. It is this: as long as your data sits as plaintext on someone else’s system, security is no longer yours to decide.**

As for keeping everything local: Phones get lost, computers fail, hard drives degrade. And in an era of cross-device sync, pure local storage means giving up backups and convenience. For most people, that is not security — it is a step backward.

The real solution is not a forced choice between insecure convenience and inconvenient security. It is this:
**Sensitive data should be encrypted the moment it is created. Encryption happens locally on your device, and only you hold the encryption key. The cloud merely stores and transports ciphertext, never seeing plaintext.**

## Before the Brain-Computer Era Arrives, Master Today’s Fundamentals

I am not sounding alarmist and claiming brain-computer interfaces will invade privacy. The technology is still nascent, and consumer-grade applications remain far off. BeiNao No.1 is currently built for medical rehabilitation, not reading the minds of ordinary people.

Still, the direction of technological progress is clear:
**Data will grow ever closer to who you are — tracking your behavior, then your body, and eventually your brain.**

Before each leap, we have one critical task:
**Build the habit of data sovereignty. Know what data is sensitive, keep sensitive data under encryption, and choose tools and services that respect your ownership of your data.**

If you do not encrypt your ID photos, bank slips and scanned contracts on your phone today, what makes you trust that neural signals collected by brain-computer devices will be handled with greater care tomorrow?

DoraCloud cannot protect your thoughts — that challenge belongs to the next decade. But it can safeguard your most important files today.

Partitioned storage, end-to-end encryption, keys in your hands.

This is the first, most tangible step you can take toward data sovereignty.

In an age where data increasingly mirrors who you are, at least make sure it always belongs to you.
