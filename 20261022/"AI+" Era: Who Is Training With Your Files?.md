# "AI+" Era: Who Is Training With Your Files?

## — As AI Reads Everything, What Should We Do About Personal Storage?

These days, two news stories make for a thought-provoking read when taken together.

**The first:** On October 20, the National Data Group was officially inaugurated in Xiong’an. Its mandate is to “lead data technology development, grow the data industry, serve artificial intelligence, and build a digital China.”

The question is: **Do your personal files count as “data”?**

## A User Agreement You May Never Have Read

Open the cloud drive you use regularly and look for its User Agreement or Terms of Service.

Most people never read them. But if you do, you may come across clauses like this:

>
> “To improve service quality, we may conduct content analysis on files uploaded by users to optimize recommendation algorithms and ad delivery.”

Put plainly: the platform reserves the right to read and analyze what you store on its cloud, and use that material to train its AI models.

This is not a conspiracy theory. It is written in black and white in the agreement.

In 2026, demand for training data for large AI models has reached an unprecedented scale. Every photo, document and voice recording can become training material.

And your cloud drive happens to be a massive, free, “legally authorized” data repository.

## What Actually Happens To Your Files?

An average person’s digital life consists of items such as:

- Photos and videos of children — a full record from birth to school
- Work documents: contracts, proposals, client information, financial statements
- Personal identification materials: scanned copies of ID cards, passports, driver’s licenses and property deeds
- Medical records: health check reports, medical histories and prescriptions
- Financial documents: bank statements, tax forms and investment records

Where are these kept today?

For most people: some sent via WeChat, some saved on cloud drives, some piled in phone photo albums, some left as email attachments.

Scattered across different platforms, each platform has rights to use them within certain limits.

**Have you ever wondered: while AI “empowers all industries”, photos of your child might end up in a model’s training dataset?**

This is no alarmist claim. The EU AI Act explicitly classifies “indiscriminate facial recognition training using publicly available internet data” as a high-risk practice. “High-risk”, however, does not mean it does not happen.

## Zero-Knowledge Is Not a Marketing Buzzword — It Is a Mathematical Principle

The fundamental difference between DoraCloud and mainstream cloud drives can be summed up in one concept: **zero-knowledge architecture.**

What zero-knowledge means:
DoraCloud’s servers store your files, but **the server side never holds the decryption keys**. Files are encrypted on your device before upload; they travel as ciphertext and remain ciphertext in storage.

Only the key in your possession can decrypt them.

What this means in practice:

- DoraCloud engineers cannot view your files. It is not that they “choose not to”; it is **technically impossible**.
- If an AI model tries to scrape your data, it only gets ciphertext — meaningless random characters to the AI.
- If servers are hacked, attackers only obtain ciphertext. Without the key, the data is worthless.
- If authorities require the platform to hand over user data, the platform can only surrender ciphertext — because it genuinely does not hold the key.

**“I cannot see your data.” — In an era where AI reads everything, this may be the most important promise a storage service can make.**

## Privacy Partition: A Separate Room For What Matters Most

DoraCloud has another often-overlooked feature: **partitioned privacy storage.**

There are two independent storage spaces: a general zone and a privacy zone, governed by separate security policies.

Use the general zone for daily work files, photo backups and study materials — items that need security but not maximum protection.

Use the privacy zone for irreplaceable, high-stakes data: password vaults, private keys, scanned IDs, financial documents and medical records.

The two zones are logically isolated and independent. A breach in one will not compromise the other.

It is like your home: the living room is accessible, the bedroom is private, and the safe sits deep inside the bedroom closet. Different data deserves different security levels.
