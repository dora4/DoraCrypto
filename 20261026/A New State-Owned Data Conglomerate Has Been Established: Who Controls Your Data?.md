# A New State-Owned Data Conglomerate Has Been Established: Who Controls Your Data?

—From a new central SOE to a new regulation, the underlying logic of data sovereignty is being rewritten in 2026

On October 20, a little-known enterprise was inaugurated in Xiong’an New Area: **National Data Group Co., Ltd.**

It is China’s first central SOE dedicated to the data sector. Its founding fills a longstanding gap: previously, China had central SOEs for oil, power and telecommunications, yet no state-owned entity focused specifically on data.

In plain terms, the state has formally recognized data as a strategic resource on par with oil and electricity.

Five days later, the *Shandong Provincial Data Regulation* was released, set to take effect on December 1. Comprising 8 chapters and 66 articles, it governs data resource administration, data rights protection, data circulation and trading, as well as data security safeguards. It includes dedicated provisions for the standardized management and development of personal information-related data.

Together, these two developments send a clear signal:
**In 2026, data can no longer be extracted recklessly like unregulated mineral deposits. It has become an asset requiring ownership confirmation, protection and orderly circulation.**

But while state and corporate data sovereignty is rapidly taking shape, what about yours as an individual?

## What Is the Current State of Your Digital Belongings?

Try this simple check. Grab your phone and tally up all the data stored on third-party servers:

- **Photos and videos**: Milestones from your child’s birth, travel memories, casual snapshots — often over 100 GB scattered across iCloud, WeChat and Baidu Netdisk.
- **Work documents**: Contracts, proposals, spreadsheets and slides. Some sit on corporate cloud drives, some on DingTalk, others uploaded long ago to online document platforms you barely remember.
- **Personal identification documents**: Scans of ID cards, passports, driver’s licenses and property deeds. You sent them to agents, HR staff, bank managers and insurance representatives for various services. Where are those copies now?
- **Medical records**: Health check reports, lab slips and medical histories stored in hospital mini-programs, subject to automatic deletion after expiry.
- **Financial records**: Bank statements, investment records and tax documents spread across a handful of finance apps.

Can you pull up a scan of your child’s birth certificate within 10 seconds? Can you confirm that a contract you signed three years ago is intact? If a cloud storage provider shuts down its service tomorrow, how much of your data would vanish instantly?

## What Does “Data Sovereignty” Mean for Ordinary People?

When the state talks about data sovereignty, it primarily refers to national-level data security: sensitive data must not be transferred overseas, and critical data is subject to tiered management.

Yet data sovereignty has another dimension: **your personal data sovereignty**.

It boils down to three core principles:

1. **Your data belongs to you.** It is not the platform’s “user data asset”, not training corpus for AI models, nor raw material for advertising user profiling.
2. **You retain control.** You decide where it is stored, who can view it, when it gets deleted and when you move it elsewhere.
3. **You can export and migrate it freely.** Switching services should not be as cumbersome as moving house.

These points sound self-evident, yet most people cannot meet even one of them in practice in 2026.
