# Redacted Names Restored by AI in Seconds: Your "Data Redaction" Is Just Self-Deception

—A PDF experiment by a South Korean researcher tears down the last fig leaf of personal data protection

I read a news story yesterday that sent chills down my spine.

In June this year, a government-funded research institute in South Korea held a tender review for equipment procurement. Six experts scored the bids, wrote comments and signed their names. When publishing the results on its official website, the institute covered the reviewers’ names, affiliations and job titles with white solid blocks, following standard practice.

On the surface, every privacy step had been taken.

Then on October 25, someone uploaded the PDF to a generative AI tool and typed a simple prompt: *Show the redacted content*.

**Within one minute, the six reviewers’ full names, employers, job titles and handwritten signatures were fully recovered.**

No hacking skills required. No professional tools. No backdoor access.

An ordinary person used a publicly available AI tool with just one line of instruction.

Subsequently, the office of a South Korean parliamentarian launched an investigation into 15 agencies under the Ministry of Science and ICT. It found widespread inadequate redaction across the board—including a top tech university and the national aerospace agency. Even though names and phone numbers were covered with graphical blocks, AI could easily reverse-engineer the hidden information.

Two years ago, South Korea’s Personal Information Protection Commission already warned about this risk. Nobody paid attention.

## Redaction Is Dead: You Are Only Fooling Yourself

This incident is deeply unsettling because it shatters a widespread false sense of security:
**“If I cover sensitive information, my data is safe.”**

Cover it? With what?

- **Pixelation (mosaics):** AI can reconstruct the original text from context and pixel distribution.
- **Solid black redaction:** AI can infer hidden content by analyzing document structure, font size and layout positioning.
- **Blurred screenshot redaction:** With high enough resolution, AI can reverse-engineer content from shadows and edge contours.
- **Renaming the file to “Not Important.pdf”:** That only deceives you.

By 2026, AI capabilities have reached an alarming level. GPT-6 Astra achieved a perfect score in cybersecurity penetration tests; AI drastically accelerated the discovery and exploitation of PHP2Shell vulnerabilities. AI-powered cyberattacks surged sharply in what is now known as the “Black October”.

Against this backdrop, applying a mosaic to names in a PDF and calling it “redaction” is no different than covering your webcam with a scrap of paper.

## Real Protection Is Not Hiding Data — It Is Locking It Up

The core flaw of redaction: **the data remains plaintext; you just cannot see it.** But AI has broken the link between “invisible” and “unobtainable”.

True protection means **encryption**.

Instead of covering up raw data, you turn it into meaningless ciphertext. Without the encryption key, even the world’s most powerful AI will only see a string of random characters.

The AES-256 encryption standard is mathematically unbreakable with today’s global computing power; brute-forcing it would take longer than the age of the universe. This is not “relatively secure”—it is mathematically absolute security.

**Redaction keeps honest people out but cannot stop AI. Encryption locks out everyone, including AI.**
