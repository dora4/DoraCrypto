# How Much Did You Spend Over National Day? Time to Check If Your Digital Spending Records Are Secure

Oct 9.

The second day after the holiday. Your Alipay bill has probably arrived — flight tickets, hotel stays, meals, scenic spot tickets, fuel, parking fees, local specialties, red envelopes… Over seven days, spending can range from 3,000–5,000 yuan to tens of thousands.

But today I’m not talking about how much you spent. I want to talk about the **digital traces** generated during your purchases: where they are stored, and whether they are safe.

## How Much Data Does One National Day Trip Generate?

Let’s do the math:

- Booking flights: ID photos uploaded to airline apps and booking platforms; payment information linked to third-party systems
- Booking hotels: Full name, phone number, check-in date and credit card data stored in hotel group databases
- Fuel payment: Bank app transaction logs accurate to the minute and location
- Scan-to-order meals: WeChat/Alipay grants merchants access to your nickname and avatar
- Scenic tickets: Real-name ticketing links your ID number to your travel route
- Parking payment: License plate number, entry/exit timestamps and payment details

Over the seven-day holiday, an average person may leave dozens of data touchpoints. These pieces of data are scattered across more than a dozen platforms and apps, all claiming “we will protect your privacy.”

But what is the reality? In September this year, the Cyberspace Administration of China announced 82 non-compliant apps, covering travel, finance, consumption and other sectors. Data breaches are not a remote possibility — they have already happened.

## Your Phone Has an "Unprotected Zone"

You took plenty of photos during the holiday. Some may include: photos of hotel room cards showing room numbers, boarding passes with barcodes that can be decoded to reveal your name and flight details, parking payment screenshots with license plates and locations, photos of your children (which many people upload to various platforms without a second thought).

Most of these photos sit in your phone gallery, mixed with selfies, food shots and landscape photos. They are not encrypted or isolated. Anyone who gets hold of your phone can view them.

This is no alarmist tale. A friend lost her phone at a scenic spot last month. Although she locked it remotely, whoever picked it up browsed her gallery in the two hours before locking. Later, she found someone had posted a screenshot of her boarding pass on WeChat Moments.

## Post-Holiday Digital Cleanup Checklist

Today is the first workday after the holiday. Take this chance to do a few simple things:
**1. Clear sensitive photos from your gallery.**
Photos of boarding passes, room cards and parking receipts are no longer needed after use, or should be moved into encrypted storage.

**2. Check your password notes.**
Many people store passwords in phone memos. If your memo app is unencrypted, those passwords are saved as plain text. Move them to DoraBox and delete the copies in your memo.

**3. Review which apps still hold your ID photos.**
You may have uploaded ID photos for flights, hotels and tickets over the holiday. Go into those apps and delete them if possible.

**4. Give financial documents a secure home.**
Bank statements, insurance policies and investment records are far more sensitive than selfies — yet many people protect them worse (at least you can set your selfies to disappear from Moments after three days).

## Spending Money Is Temporary; Protecting Your Data Is Lifelong

Money spent during National Day will be replenished when your next salary arrives. But leaked ID numbers, bank card details and home addresses — once exposed, cannot be fixed in a month or two.

DoraBox does not turn you into a privacy fanatic. It simply helps you store your most important data in the right place.

You keep miscellaneous items in one drawer at home, but valuables go into a safe. Your phone needs this separation too.

Make your first post-holiday task: tidy up your digital life.
