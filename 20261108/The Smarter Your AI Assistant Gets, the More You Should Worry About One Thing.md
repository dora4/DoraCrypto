# The Smarter Your AI Assistant Gets, the More You Should Worry About One Thing

## — When AI Agents Start Rummaging Through Your Files, Who Guards What You Want Kept Private?

---

Beijing released an official document today.

Its full title is lengthy: *Several Measures of Beijing Municipality on Accelerating Development Led by AI Agents*. Its core message is concise: **Over the next three years, Beijing will strongly support the AI agent industry.**

These are not just chatbots that answer questions on demand. They are “digital employees” capable of autonomous planning, independent execution and tool invocation.

The policy introduces a concept called “research agents”. Their functions include independently identifying scientific problems, designing experimental protocols, calling lab instruments and analyzing results.

It sounds promising. But replace “research” with “daily life”, and the picture changes:

Your AI assistant sorts your emails, files your contracts, pulls last year’s medical reports to compare them with this year’s, fetches your pay slips and spending records from last month, and calculates how much you can save this month.

While doing all of this, it gains access to every single one of your files.

**Every email. Every contract. Every medical report. Every bank screenshot.**

You grant it permission — because without access, it cannot work. Yet the moment you authorize it, your entire digital life lies open to an AI system.

## A Growing Paradox

2026 is the breakout year for AI agents. Starting with Manus early this year and followed by a wave of agent platforms, the idea of “letting AI handle your work” is no longer a vision — it is everyday reality.

Yet a paradox emerges:
**The more capable an AI agent becomes, the more data it needs to access. The more data it accesses, the larger your privacy exposure surface.**

This is not a question of whether you should use AI. You cannot reject intelligent tools entirely over privacy concerns, just as you would not stay indoors forever to avoid traffic accidents.

The real question is: **Can you isolate what you want kept private while enjoying AI’s convenience?**

An analogy: you hire a housekeeper to clean your home. They need access to the living room, kitchen and bathroom. But they do not need — and should not be able — to open your safe, your diary or your locked drawer.

**The issue is not whether you trust the housekeeper. It is whether you separate sensitive items from ordinary ones.**

## Most People Store Everything Piled on the Table

I have observed how people around me use cloud storage.

The conclusion is sobering: **Most users mix all types of files together.**

Photos, videos, work slides, scanned ID documents, bank statements, a child’s birth certificate, last year’s tax records, personal IOUs — all sit in one folder. Some users go one small step further and split files into “Work”, “Life” and “Photos”, yet scanned ID documents still share the “Life” folder with travel guides.

It is like leaving your diary next to takeout menus on the coffee table. The housekeeper is not actively snooping; they simply wipe the table, and your private writing comes into view.

The same applies to AI agents. If you tell it to “organize this folder”, it must read every file inside. If you ask it to “find a contract from last year”, it scans your entire storage space.

**It is not acting maliciously. You left the door open.**

## The Industrial Bank of China Case

Two days ago, the National Financial Regulatory Administration published a batch of penalty notices.

Among them, Industrial Bank of China was fined **6.95 million yuan for inadequate data security management.**

6.95 million yuan. A listed bank with hundreds of billions in annual revenue received this penalty due to lapses in data security.

You may wonder: what does a bank have to do with my personal files?

Here is the connection: **If a bank equipped with professional IT teams, regulatory compliance obligations and massive budgets can fail at data security management, what makes you think your free cloud drive is more secure?**

The honest answer is that you do not. You simply have not considered the risk.

## The Value of Segmentation

Back to the housekeeper analogy.

Physical homes have natural zones: living rooms are shared spaces, bedrooms are semi-private, and a safe is fully restricted. Different items belong in different zones with distinct access rules.

In the digital world, however, most people skip this segmentation.

## Why This Matters Even More in the AI Era

Before AI agents, file breaches mainly happened in two ways: hacker intrusions and insider leaks.

AI agents introduce a third pathway: **over-access under legitimate authorization.**

You are not hacked. You grant access voluntarily. Your AI assistant is not malware — it genuinely helps you. But in the process, it inevitably encounters information you never intended it to see.

If such sensitive data is stored on the provider’s servers as plaintext or encrypted with keys controlled by the provider, these risks follow:

- The provider’s AI systems may analyze your file content to “improve agent performance”
- Provider engineers could theoretically view your files during system maintenance
- Your files may be exposed if the provider suffers a breach
- Revised service terms may change how your data can be used

These are not hypothetical risks. Multiple incidents in 2026 have confirmed private user files winding up in AI training datasets, because users agreed to data usage buried within platform terms of service.

**DoraCloud’s Private Data Zone keeps your sensitive files out of this AI data pipeline from the start.**
Keys stay in your possession; ciphertext sits on the server. The provider’s AI only sees meaningless noise, not your contracts or medical reports.
