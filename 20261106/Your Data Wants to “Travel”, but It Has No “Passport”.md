# Your Data Wants to “Travel”, but It Has No “Passport”

## — From Wuzhen to Peking University: A Century-Old Dilemma of Data Flow and One Person’s Filing Cabinet

Autumn in Wuzhen is arguably the season with the highest concentration of China’s internet industry.

On November 2, the 2026 World Internet Conference “Light of the Internet” Expo opened in Wuzhen, themed **“Smart Connectivity, Wisdom Shaping the Future”**. The most eye-catching section in the exhibition hall was “Tech for Good · New Security”. Staff demonstrated a chilling operation on site: establishing a connection with a mobile phone via Bluetooth protocol, within just a few seconds, SMS messages and contacts were leaked and synced to a nearby screen.

The expressions of onlookers shifted from curiosity to unease.

Elsewhere in the same hall, quantum secure messaging systems, AI attack-defense platforms and simulation and deduction systems were showcased one after another. They all tackle the same question:
**When smart connectivity links everything together, how can data flow without being “seen”?**

But if you are not in Wuzhen, what does this have to do with you?

A great deal. Because this very challenge is unfolding much closer to you.

## The Head of a Hospital’s Dilemma

Three days after the expo opened, on November 5, Peking University Health Science Center and a research institute jointly launched a data laboratory.

At the launch ceremony, an expert put it plainly:
**“We dare not share, and we do not know how to share.”**

These six words perfectly sum up the core dilemma in healthcare data. China holds massive volumes of high-quality medical data locked inside individual hospitals — imaging scans, digital pathology slides, clinical trial datasets and patient medication records. This data carries enormous research value, yet cross-institutional data circulation has nearly ground to a halt.

The reasons are straightforward:

- Data formats vary across hospitals, making datasets incompatible even when shared
- No clear liability framework for security breaches once data leaves a hospital
- Patient private information risks improper use during transmission
- Existing technology struggles to strike a balance between sharing and protection

The result? Vast troves of valuable medical data are trapped in isolated data silos, growing stale over time and eventually turning into neglected “digital waste”.

Experts note that real-world research and public health policymaking demand ever-higher data quality and larger sample sizes — needs that current setups cannot meet.

**The data wants to travel, but it has no passport.**

## What This Means for You

You may not run a hospital or work in research, but the phrase “dare not share, do not know how to share” will feel familiar.

Open the file manager on your phone and take stock:

- Photos of your ID card — sent to a landlord weeks ago, still sitting in chat history
- Payslip screenshots — emailed to a bank last month for a loan application
- A PDF medical report — shared with an insurance agent recently
- Scanned contracts — photographed and sent to the other party after signing

Every time these files “go out”, you feel a quiet sense of unease:
**Will they keep it? Forward it? Use it for something else?**

Still you send them. Because without sharing, the task cannot get done.

This is the personal version of the data flow dilemma:
**Your files gain value only when they move — yet every transfer opens you up to risk.**

## The “Legitimate” Channel for Data Leaks

When people hear “data breach”, most picture hackers. But the reality in 2026 is this:
**Most leaks are not “stolen”; they are “seen”.**

IBM’s *2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report* states the global average cost of a breach has hit 4.99 million US dollars, up 12% year on year and reaching an all-time high. More notable than the figure itself is the shifting breakdown of breach causes:
**Insider leaks — intentional or accidental — are overtaking external attacks as the top source of data exposure.**

Consider what this means. Your files are accessed “legitimately”: by colleagues, service providers, system administrators or AI indexing tools. These parties have authorized access, yet they can view far more than you intended.

Why? Most file management tools are built around one design:
**One entry point, one password — and once inside, everything is visible.**

It is like hiring a cleaner and giving them a key to your whole house. They may only clean the living room, but they have the ability to walk into your bedroom, open your drawers and read your diary. The problem is not whether they will do it — it is that **you have no way of knowing if they did.**

## Segmentation: A Severely Underestimated Security Strategy

The security solutions on show in Wuzhen — quantum communication, AI cyber defense, simulation platforms — are mostly infrastructure-grade tools built for enterprises and governments. Ordinary users cannot access or afford them.

One principle, however, applies to everyone:
**segmented management.**

At the expo, different exhibits are placed in separate zones. Precision instruments sit in independent temperature- and humidity-controlled display cases, while ordinary exhibits occupy open booths. Not because precision gear is inherently “more important”, but because **their security requirements differ.**

The same logic applies to your files.

Your travel photos and bank statements have completely different risk profiles. If photos leak, you may only feel annoyed for an afternoon; leaked financial records can leave you exposed to targeted phishing for a whole year. Still, most people store them in the same place under the same protection level.

That leaves only two bad options: encrypt everything or encrypt nothing. Full encryption is slow and cumbersome — you will abandon it by day three. No encryption is convenient, yet leaves you fully exposed.

**This is a false binary.**

The same challenge applies to individual users.

Your files do not become valuable only when locked away. On the contrary:
**Files must move, must be used, and must be available where and when they are needed.**
But movement does not mean exposure, and access does not mean unrestricted viewing.

DoraCloud creates that balance between flow and protection.
