# Meta’s AI Chat Bot Just Reset Your “Friend’s” Password

Early in 2026, Meta’s AI chatbot made headlines for all the wrong reasons.

Researchers discovered that by simply chatting with Meta’s AI assistant and pretending to be a user locked out of their account, attackers could trick the AI into sending a password reset code to any email address they specified. No identity verification required, no security questions to answer, not even the original password.

Thousands of Instagram accounts were hijacked this way.

The irony is stark: attackers didn’t use sophisticated hacking exploits. All they did was hold a conversation with an AI.

Months have passed since this story broke. Yet the underlying problem has not eased — it is accelerating.

## “Cloud Storage” Does Not Equal “Secure” — A Concept to Re-examine

Many people equate storing files “on the cloud” with being safe.

Cloud storage is indeed more reliable than USB drives and more convenient than local hard disks. But reliability and security are not the same thing.

Reliability means your data won’t disappear — servers have backups, redundancy and disaster recovery. Security means unauthorized parties cannot view your data — and this is where most cloud providers fall short.

Among cybersecurity enforcement cases released by China’s Cyberspace Administration in 2026: a hospital in Henan exposed patient records via a weak password vulnerability; a tech firm in Shanghai collected user data beyond permitted scope and transmitted it to overseas data centers; a hardware product from Guangdong transferred personal information with zero encryption.

**Whether your files stay safe on the cloud hinges on three things: who can access them, whether data is encrypted, and whether the service provider is trustworthy.**

## Segmented Storage: Separate Work from Private Data

DoraCloud’s first design principle is **segmentation**.

Your cloud space is split into isolated security zones: a general file area and a private data vault, governed by separate encryption policies and access permissions.

Store work documents, public materials and routine backups in the general zone for easy access. Put ID documents, bank details, medical records and private photos in the private zone, which requires extra identity verification to unlock.

This is no fancy gimmick, but it solves a real-world problem:
**Most people store photos of their ID cards alongside casual travel selfies in the same cloud drive.**

If a breach happens, losing selfies is harmless. If ID photos fall into the hands of cybercriminals, the consequences can be catastrophic.

## Client-Side Encryption: Lock Files On Your Phone Before Uploading

DoraCloud’s second core principle is **client-side encryption**.

Files are encrypted locally on your phone before they are uploaded. What reaches the server is ciphertext — only your unique key can decrypt it.

This differs drastically from mainstream cloud drives. Most cloud services only encrypt data in transit (to block interception). Once files arrive on servers, they are stored in plaintext or in a form controllable by the platform. That means the provider itself can view your files, authorities can compel the provider to hand over data, and hackers who breach the server can read everything directly.

The logic of client-side encryption is simple:
**You apply the lock on your end, and only you hold the key.** Even if servers are compromised, attackers only get unreadable scrambled data.

## Principle of Least Privilege: Apps Should Not Access Data They Don’t Need

Open your phone settings and check permissions granted to your apps. A weather app asks for your contacts. A flashlight app requests location access. A file manager demands microphone permission.

DoraCloud follows the **Principle of Least Privilege**. It only requests the file access permissions required to run. It never touches your contacts, reads SMS messages, requests location, or listens to your microphone.

More importantly, it does not collect metadata related to file content. What you store, what you view, what you share — all of this stays on your local device, leaving no trace on DoraCloud servers.
