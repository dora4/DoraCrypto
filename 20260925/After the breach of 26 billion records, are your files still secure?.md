## Three hidden risks of cloud storage

Most people choose cloud storage for its convenience, low cost and sufficient capacity. Yet few carefully assess the following risks:
**Risk 1: Server-side data breaches.** Data on mainstream cloud drives is hosted on the service provider’s servers. If servers are hacked, staff misuse access privileges, or security vulnerabilities emerge on the provider’s end, your files may be exposed. This is not hypothetical — multiple well-known cloud storage services have suffered user data leaks in recent years.

**Risk 2: Mixed storage of sensitive and ordinary files.** Your scanned ID card sits alongside family travel photos in the same cloud folder, protected by identical security controls. These two types of data, however, require vastly different security levels. Applying a one-size-fits-all approach to sensitive and regular files is a common security blind spot.

**Risk 3: Overreaching permissions and excessive data collection.** Many cloud storage services reserve broad rights to access user data in their terms of service, for content moderation, ad targeting, or even "service improvement". The contents of your files may be read and analysed within what is framed as legal boundaries.

## Practical steps: 5 actions you can take today

Regardless of which cloud storage service you ultimately use, start these five steps immediately:

1. **Take inventory of sensitive files.** Separate ID photos, contracts, bank statements and password records from regular folders for dedicated management.
2. **Review privacy policies of your current cloud drive.** Focus on: whether the provider retains access rights to your data; if data is used for advertising or user profiling; what notification procedures apply in the event of a data breach.
3. **Enable two-factor authentication (where available).** Many cloud services support TOTP secondary verification, which drastically reduces account hijacking risks.
4. **Keep local backups of critical files.** The cloud should never be your sole copy. Maintain at least one local backup on hardware you physically control.
5. **Consider a tiered storage setup.** Use standard cloud storage for everyday files, and encrypted storage for sensitive materials — such as the privacy partition in Dora Cloud, or the higher-grade Dora Box encryption solution.
