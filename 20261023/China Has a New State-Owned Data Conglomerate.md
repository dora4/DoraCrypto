# China Has a New State-Owned Data Conglomerate — Who Is in Charge of Your Data?

*—Starting with this new central SOE, a discussion on the ownership of personal data*

On October 20, headline news swept the tech community: **the National Data Group was inaugurated in Xiong’an New Area**, listed as the 28th enterprise on the roster of central SOEs.

This is far more than trivial news. It signals that at the national level, data is now treated as a strategic resource on par with oil, electricity and telecommunications, ready for large-scale operation. The market-based allocation of data factors now has its national team.

The macro agenda is sweeping: interconnecting data infrastructure, advancing data innovation, enabling regulated data trading and circulation… all of these bear directly on national development and public wellbeing.

Today, however, I want to zoom in on a smaller question:
**Who looks after your own personal data?**

## An Overlooked Shift

Over the past five years, China has steadily strengthened personal information protection:

- In 2026, **more than 4,000 mobile apps were ordered to rectify personal information compliance issues**
- Global user data deletion requests surged **567% compared with 2021**
- **145 new AI-related regulations** were introduced worldwide
- 26 billion records were exposed in the “Mother of All Breaches” breach incident

The numbers keep rising, and regulations keep tightening — yet one thing remains unchanged:
**Most people still protect their data the same way they did five years ago.**

The state is building a secure data “highway” for society. But the narrow path leading from your front door onto that highway may still be an unpaved dirt track.

---

## What State Are Your Files In Right Now?

Let’s run a simple thought experiment.

Suppose your favourite cloud storage service — whatever its name — announced tomorrow that it would shut down. What would happen to your files?

- Could you download everything within 24 hours? (Many users store tens or hundreds of gigabytes, which would take days to retrieve.)
- Would the downloaded files still open properly? (Some cloud services wrap content in proprietary formats.)
- Would all the share links you sent out stop working? (They would.)
- Would your file version history still exist? (Most likely not.)

Now a more serious scenario.

If that cloud provider’s servers were hacked — not a hypothetical; this has happened repeatedly in recent years — what would become of your ID photos, scanned contracts and financial statements stored there?

**The honest answer: you would not know. You may not even be sure whether the provider would notify you.**

That is the fundamental reality of storing data on someone else’s platform: you hand over control, without receiving ironclad guarantees in return.

The National Data Group governs how data factors flow, are priced and protected at the national level. It builds the foundational infrastructure.

But personal data security ultimately rests with you. The state can build the power grid, yet whether the light bulb in your home stays on depends on whether you pay your bill and maintain your wiring.

**DoraCloud is built to pave that last stretch of the road for you.**

Three core points set it apart from mainstream cloud drives:

**First, your files are encrypted before they reach the cloud.**
Encryption does not happen on the server side after upload. It takes place locally on your device, using keys only you hold. What arrives on the server is unintelligible ciphertext. Even if the server is compromised, attackers only get unusable encrypted data.

**Second, sensitive private files are isolated from ordinary files.**
DoraCloud supports partitioned storage: everyday photos and work documents live in standard storage space; ID documents, contracts and financial records go into an encrypted vault. The two zones use separate encryption and independent access controls. Think of it as a wardrobe and a safe in your home — you would not keep your passport mixed in with your T-shirts.

**Third, your data is never locked in.**
It uses open formats and standard protocols for easy export, migration and backup. DoraCloud avoids proprietary wrapping and does not create vendor lock-in. Your data belongs to you, and you can take it away anytime.

## An Underrated Habit

Whether you end up using DoraCloud or not, here is something you can do today:

**Sort the files on your phone and computer into two piles, based on whether their loss would hurt you.**

The low-stakes pile — casual photos, work drafts, general backups — can safely stay on ordinary cloud storage. It is convenient, affordable and fit for purpose.

The high-stakes pile — ID scans, contracts, bank statements, password notes — deserves safer custody. This could be an encrypted external drive, the DoraCloud vault, or any other trusted encryption solution.

The golden rule: **keep them separated.**

This is one of the oldest, most reliable principles in information security: tiered storage. It is simple, yet most people never put it into practice.
